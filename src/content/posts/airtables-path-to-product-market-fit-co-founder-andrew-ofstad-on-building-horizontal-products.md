## Summary
Here is the summary of the document "Airtable’s path to product-market fit — co-founder Andrew Ofstad on building horizontal products":

1. **Founding Story**: Airtable's co-founders met at Duke University and shared a common interest in programming and technology, which led them to start the company.

2. **Early Vision**: The initial vision for Airtable was to democratize software creation,  inspired by historical advances in computing such as the GUI by Xerox PARC and Apple's Macintosh, making it easier for non-programmers to build software.

3. **Contrarian Approach**: Unlike the lean startup methodology, Airtable focused on deep research and understanding of the software creation space before launching.

4. **First Prototype**: The initial prototype was a simple, user-friendly database that looked like a spreadsheet but with additional functionalities like typed fields and linked tables.

5. **Product Iterations**: Early versions focused on refining the user interface and experience, making database concepts more accessible and visual.

6. **Early Users**: Initial users were a mix of those who intuitively understood the database concepts and those who needed templates and guidance to see its potential.

7. **Launch Strategy**: Airtable had a gradual launch process, starting with a private alpha, moving to a beta phase, and finally a public launch on Hacker News.

8. **Product Market Fit**: The realization of product-market fit came when large companies like WeWork adopted Airtable, using it widely across their teams.

9. **Pricing Strategy**: Early on, Airtable included a pricing page to signal that it would be a paid product, even before the billing system was fully developed.

10. **Competition**: Airtable focused on its own vision and product development, learning from the ecosystem without obsessing over direct competitors.

11. **Go-to-Market Strategy**: Airtable started with a broad, horizontal approach and later refined its focus to specific use cases and industries based on user adoption patterns.

12. **Sales and Customer Success**: Early sales and customer success teams played a crucial role in understanding customer needs and translating them into product improvements.

13. **Future Vision**: Airtable aims to become a connected apps platform, enabling teams to build and deploy custom software while integrating with organizational data to break down silos.

14. **Enterprise Focus**: Emphasis on making the product easier to use and focusing on large enterprises to support mission-critical processes.

15. **Scaling Challenges**: Balancing the complexity of the product for different user personas and maintaining a consumer-grade experience while scaling for enterprise use.

16. **Long-term Planning**: Airtable employs a three-year vision and OKR (Objectives and Key Results) framework to align its strategic initiatives and day-to-day execution.

17. **Shift from Consumer to Enterprise**: Initially considered a consumer use case, Airtable shifted focus to workplace use where collaborative databases add significant value.

This summary encapsulates the key points and insights shared by Andrew Ofstad about Airtable's journey and strategies in building a successful horizontal product.

About WeWork using Airtable:
> Speaker 1: "Where I really felt like we had product market fit is when we had our first customer where the customer was WeWork. We landed with them and it grew virally within the company and went more or less well... I visited WeWork and I looked around and everybody's computer monitor had Airtable open and I was like, 'Oh my God, this is actually a thing. People use it.' It became so much more real at that point. That's when we started to see product market fit is both when we saw a few companies where we expanded wall to wall but also certain industries and functions. So for example, media, we were being used for video production a lot, marketing teams, a lot of companies we were being used, seeing repeatable cases of us landing within those industries and functions and then expanding within the company. That's the growth engine that we live by today."
