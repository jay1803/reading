Best firms excel at three interfaces: GTM-product, product-dev and dev-ops

Four functions are needed to build and distribute business software: product definition, development, operation and Go-To-Market (GTM) (GTM includes marketing, sales, success and service).
### The modern GTM-product interface
Best GTM-product interface practices are:
1. Build features that acquire customers
2. Use self-service onboarding to activate customers
3. Use PQLs to qualify customers
4. Build-in purchasing for customers
5. Adopt monthly payments & consumption based pricing to bill customers
6. Build features to retain and expand customers
7. Establish a product community to learn from customers
### The modern product-dev interface
Best product-dev interface practices are:
1. Build stream-aligned teams: focus on outcome, not output.
2. Architect microservices for teams: use a microservice architecture to cut applications into small chunks of independent services.
3. Establish continuous discovery & delivery: set-up every team with one dedicated product manager and establish a dual-track agile philosophy with continuous discovery and delivery.
4. Empower the teams: create a product vision that’s aligned with your customers and market trends. Use this product vision as your north star, to provide direction to your teams.
5. Enforce data-driven decisions: use the modern data stack (MDS) to connect product and development data.
### The modern dev-ops interface
Best Dev-Ops interface practices are:
1. Use low-code/no-code (LC/NC) to expand offering
2. Establish serverless-first thinking
3. Use containers for constant load
4. Establish progressive delivery: manage risk by avoiding big-bang deployments. Deliver changes to small audiences first, and then expand to larger and riskier audiences, validating the results as you go.
5. Decouple deployments from releases: introduce an additional layer for functional and behavioral tests with Feature Flags (FF), to decouple deployment from the release.
6. Introduce DevSecOps from day one
7. Standardize the observability stack: a modern microservice-based application is a complex distributed system with abstractions including FaaS, BaaS, and infrastructure-as-code, and Kubernetes to deploy and run these microservices.
8. Automate AI deployments with MLOps: building machine learning models is getting easier, but translating them from science projects into reliable, scalable software is hard.
9. Establish continuous cost optimization: application developers generate cloud cost with every line of code they program, whether using LC/NC, serverless, or containers.
10. Use chaos engineering in SRE: microservice-based applications are inherently complex.
