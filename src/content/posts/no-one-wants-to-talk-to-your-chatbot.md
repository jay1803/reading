### Party Like It’s 1999
We imagined that users would create direct relationships with our URLs. We imagined there would be brand loyalty. We scrambled to buy meaningful domain names.

We were the apps and the browser was the new OS.

But of course, that isn’t how it worked out. It was the search enginess that became to the entry point for users. We watched most users launch their browsers to Google and then search for us.

In response, we have created SEO
### Rise of the Apps
In 2007...iPhone launched. There had been other mobile platforms that came earlier, but none that reached the scale of the iTunes App Store (Now just the App Store).
We had our icons on the screen. Users launched us directly.

Apps were the new applications and iOS/Android was the new OS.
### Apps are not Applications.
The OS handles payments, security, sharing, etc. As more features get handled by the OS, the more they become first point of contact with the user. The more the user has a relationship with their device and less with their apps.
### Rise of the Assistants
In 2010, Apple launched Siri on MacOS
if you look at the OS APIs, each company has been creating deeper integrations with their virtual personal assistants and their hosted Apps.
Users talk to Alexa and skills respond back through her and in her voice.
### Language Has Been the Logjam
Up until now, all of these virtual assistants have been held back by their lack of language skills.
Suddenly with Large Language Models (LLM) and each of these companies running their own LLM as a Service
### The Relationship Is With The Assistant
With LLM, these personal virtual assistants are the new starting point. They will be to apps what Google and Bing are to the web.

Few people will be willing to interact with an army of different chatbots and online assistants.

If you have a chatbot, it is for Sir or Alexa to use, not people. I am here to tell you, no human wants to talk to your chatbot.
