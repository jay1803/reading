Here is a summary of the document **"Negative Partisanship: Why Americans Dislike Parties But Behave Like Rabid Partisans"** by Alan I. Abramowitz and Steven W. Webster:

1. **Definition of Negative Partisanship**: Americans increasingly align against one party rather than supporting the other, leading to stronger partisan behaviors despite disliking both parties.

2. **Declining Popularity of Both Parties**: Since 2000, approval ratings of both Democrats and Republicans have fallen, yet voter loyalty and party-line voting have reached record highs.

3. **Impact on Voting Behavior**: Negative partisanship has significantly reduced ticket-splitting, leading to nationalized elections where down-ballot results strongly correlate with presidential outcomes.

4. **Presidential Approval Polarization**: The partisan divide in presidential approval ratings has widened, reaching record levels under recent presidents, particularly Donald Trump.

5. **Racial Divide as a Key Driver**: The increasing racial alignment of political parties, with minorities favoring Democrats and white conservatives shifting to Republicans, has fueled negative partisanship.

6. **Media's Role in Polarization**: The rise of partisan-friendly media, including Fox News and liberal alternatives, has reinforced negative views of the opposing party.

7. **Personality and Partisanship**: Certain personality traits (e.g., agreeableness, extraversion, and emotional stability) correlate with lower levels of negative partisanship, while others fuel stronger partisan hostility.

8. **Electoral Consequences**: Negative partisanship has increased voter turnout, but it also contributes to polarization, legislative gridlock, and declining trust in government institutions.

9. **The 2016 Election and Trump’s Victory**: Despite low favorability ratings for both Trump and Clinton, partisans overwhelmingly voted for their party’s candidate due to strong dislike of the opposition.

10. **Future Implications**: Negative partisanship is likely to persist, shaping elections, governance, and public trust, with potential long-term consequences for American democracy.
