## Summary
It is about how travel tours and experiences booking websites can increase their credibility and trustworthiness by providing links to external sources that validate their claims. The article argues that users are more likely to trust reviews, ratings, awards and endorsements from third-party sources than from the website itself. The article also provides some examples of how to implement this strategy effectively.

Okay, here is a summary of each section of the article:

- Introduction: The author introduces the topic of linking to third-party sources of reviews, ratings, awards and endorsements as a way to increase credibility and trustworthiness for travel tours and experiences booking websites. The author explains that users are often skeptical about the quality and authenticity of the tours and experiences offered by these websites, and that they rely on external validation to make their decisions. The author also states that this article is based on Baymard’s large-scale testing of Travel Tours and Experiences Booking sites1.
- Why Linking to Third-Party Sources Is Important: The author provides some examples of how users react to different types of reviews, ratings, awards and endorsements on travel tours and experiences booking websites. The author shows that users are more likely to trust third-party sources than self-reported ones, especially when they are unfamiliar with the website or the tour provider. The author also points out some common pitfalls that websites should avoid when displaying these elements, such as using vague or generic terms, hiding negative reviews or ratings, or providing insufficient information about the source.
- How to Link to Third-Party Sources Effectively: The author gives some best practices for linking to third-party sources of reviews, ratings, awards and endorsements on travel tours and experiences booking websites. The author suggests that websites should use clear and descriptive labels for these elements, provide direct links to the source pages where possible, include relevant details such as dates or locations when applicable, and use visual cues such as logos or icons to indicate the source type. The author also provides some examples of how different websites implement these practices successfully.
