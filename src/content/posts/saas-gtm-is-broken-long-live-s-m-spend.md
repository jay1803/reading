### Introduction
### The Broken SaaS GTM Landscape
#### 1. Efficiency Gains
Jason Lemkin, in his [2024 State of SaaS report](https://www.saastr.com/2024-state-of-saas-with-saastr-ceo-and-founder-jason-lemkin/), highlights an interesting trend: companies are becoming more efficient in their operations.

Revenue of $300K/US employee has started to become the new normal.

#### 2. Declining Growth
David Spitz, in a candid [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dspitz_saas-gtm-metrics-activity-7139999788342669313-m_94/), points out that growth rates are dwindling.
#### 3. Rising S&M Costs
#### 4. The Self-Serve Economy
TrustRadius’s [2023 B2B Buying Disconnect](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/2023-b2b-disconnect) report reveals a fascinating shift: over 95% of B2B buyers now prefer self-serve options.

Buyers want to explore, evaluate, and make decisions independently. This self-serve economy disrupts the traditional GTM model.
#### 5. Linear Scaling Constraints
David Boyce’s reminder that “[Human GTM can only scale linearly](https://daveboyce.substack.com/p/14-how-to-get-started-in-plg-mindset)” hits the nail on the head. As companies grow, they hire more salespeople, expand marketing efforts, and invest in outreach. However, this linear scaling approach eventually hits a ceiling. Human resources are finite, and scaling beyond a certain point becomes impractical.
### The Paradox: Why More S&M Spending?
Why are SaaS companies increasing their S&M spending despite knowing that massive S&M spend is a primary cause of inefficiency?
1. Market Competition: The SaaS landscape is fiercely competitive.
2. Customer Acquisition: While self-serve options are gaining popularity, designing and building a SaaS product for PLG is non-trivial.
3. Legacy Mindset: The traditional sales playbook is deeply ingrained. Sales teams are seen as revenue engines, and marketing campaigns as essential for brand visibility. Breaking away from this mindset is challenging.
4. Investor Expectations: The market closely monitors S&M spending. Salesforce, for instance, allocates around 45% of its revenue to S&M, which raises eyebrows. Striking a balance between growth and efficiency is a delicate dance. Most times, VC investments drive the SaaS companies to adopt the Sales-led acquisition playbook. And no doubt, it works well, until it doesn’t.
### GTM options
1. Low volume, High CAC, Sales-led approach to acquiring customers. Based on the numbers, this seems to be the predominant playbook.
2. High Volume, Low CAC. PLG approach to acquiring customers

### Conclusion
As we navigate this new era, embrace the self-serve economy, explore innovative channels, and find ways to scale beyond linear constraints.
