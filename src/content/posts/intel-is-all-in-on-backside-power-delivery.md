## Summarized by BingChat
Intel is launching a new technology called PowerVia that combines backside power delivery and RibbonFET, a new type of transistor. PowerVia removes the power interconnects from above the silicon and places them below it, creating more room for the data interconnects above. This improves the performance, efficiency, and reliability of the chips, as well as reduces the cost and size of the transistors. Intel has tested PowerVia-enabled cores and found that they achieved more than a 6 percent frequency boost, more compact designs, and 30 percent less power loss. PowerVia also met the design and manufacturing criteria for high-volume production, such as matching the yield of Intel 4 chips, keeping the transistors and power interconnects working well, and enabling effective debugging. PowerVia is expected to be ready for Intel’s next-generation CPUs in 2024, and for its customers’ products in 2025. Intel aims to be the first to offer both nanosheet transistors and backside power delivery, giving it an edge over its rivals.
