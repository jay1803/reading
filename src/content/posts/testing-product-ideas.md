#### The AFTER Framework
AFTER:
- Assessment - Quick and rough evaluation of the potential and risk of an idea with no external research
- Fact Finding - Actively looking for data and facts that may support or refute the validity of the idea
- Tests - Putting increasingly more evolved versions of the idea in front of users and measuring their reactions.
- Experiments - Conducting quantitative tests that control against false results.
- Release results - Gradually releasing the product to more users and customers while monitoring the reaction.

#### Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
The term Minimum Viable Product was first used by aeronautical engineer turned business consultant, Frank Robinson in 2001. Robinson argued that companies should develop a product that is “big enough to cause adoption, satisfaction and sales, but not so big as to be bloated and risky.”[3] The MVP was designed to create “maximum ROI divided by risk.” In other words, he defines an MVP is a fully-fledged product that maximizes outcomes yet minimizes output.

## Summary
1. **Introduction**: Emphasizes the importance of validating product ideas due to the high failure rate of untested concepts and the necessity of evidence-based decision-making.
2. **What to Validate**: Focuses on validating value, usability, feasibility, and viability to ensure ideas meet market needs and are practical to implement.
3. **The AFTER Framework**: Introduces a structured approach to validation using Assessment, Fact Finding, Tests, Experiments, and Release results.
4. **Assessment Techniques**: Encourages quick evaluations to filter out weak ideas early without extensive research.
5. **Goals Alignment**: Stresses the need for product ideas to align with company goals, using tools like OKRs for clarity and focus.
6. **ICE Analysis**: Utilizes the ICE method to score ideas based on Impact, Confidence, and Ease to provide a structured evaluation.
7. **Business Modeling**: Ensures ideas are financially viable and supported by necessary resources and partnerships using business model canvases.
8. **Assumption Mapping**: Identifies and prioritizes risks and assumptions to focus validation efforts effectively.
9. **Stakeholder Reviews**: Engages internal stakeholders early to identify potential risks and opposition, aiming for collaborative mitigation strategies.
10. **Fact Finding and Data Analysis**: Gathers data from various sources to support or refute the validity of an idea, using user behavior analytics and feedback.
11. **User/Customer Interviews**: Conducts interviews to gain qualitative insights into user needs and validate assumptions through direct interaction.
12. **Field Research**: Observes users in their natural environment to understand behavior and context, employing methods like ethnographic interviewing and contextual inquiry.
13. **Surveys**: Uses online surveys to quickly gather broad user feedback, being cautious of biases and ensuring questions are clear and concise.
14. **Competitor Analysis**: Analyzes competitors to learn from their successes and mistakes, but cautions against blindly copying their strategies  .
15. **Testing Methods**: Implements various tests, including Minimum Viable Products (MVPs), smoke tests, and usability tests, to iteratively validate ideas with users      .
16. **Human Operated Tests**: Simulates functionality manually before automation to quickly validate concepts with minimal development  .
17. **Longitudinal User Studies**: Measures user reactions over time to assess the sustained value and satisfaction of a product .
18. **Early Adopter Programs**: Engages early adopters for feedback and support, building advocacy and refining the product before a wider release  .
19. **Internal Company Tests**: Tests products internally (dogfooding) to identify bugs and usability issues early, fostering internal support  .
20. **Labs and Beta Programs**: Uses labs and beta versions to test incomplete features with users who opt-in, gathering early feedback and refining the product   .
21. **Experiments and A/B Tests**: Conducts controlled experiments to compare user responses and validate changes, ensuring statistical significance   .
22. **Release Tests**: Monitors user reactions during gradual rollouts to ensure stability and user satisfaction, making adjustments as needed  .
23. **Interpreting Results**: Analyzes test results critically to decide on the next steps, using the Gilad Confidence Meter to gauge the strength of evidence   .
24. **Systematic Validation with GIST**: Advocates for a structured system (GIST framework) to guide idea collection, validation, and implementation .
