The average professional checks their email [15 times a day](https://hbr.org/2019/01/how-to-spend-way-less-time-on-email-every-day) – which gives you plenty of chances to reach prospects or market to current customers.

One key strategy to boost email deliverability and engagement is the process of email warm up.
### What is Email Warm Up?
Email warm-up refers to the process of establishing a positive sender reputation for an email address prior to sending out email campaigns.

In a nutshell, email warm-up is about gradually increasing the volume of emails sent from a new or dormant email account, allowing it to gain a positive reputation with Internet Service Providers (ISPs). This approach helps prevent your emails from being marked as spam and increases the likelihood of reaching your recipients’ inboxes.
### How Email Warm Up Works
Here’s how the process works:
- Build a Positive IP and Domain Reputation: The reputation of your IP address and domain plays a vital role in email warm-up. Building a positive reputation involves sending consistent, high-quality emails that receive positive engagement from recipients.
- Ramp Up Sending Frequency Over Time: Rather than abruptly sending a large volume of emails, warm-up involves gradually increasing your email volume over time. This approach helps avoid triggering spam filters and establishes credibility with email service providers.
- Track Engagement Metrics: Monitoring engagement metrics like open rates, click-through rates, and replies during the warm-up process is essential. Positive engagement signals to email service providers that your emails are valuable and desired by recipients, contributing to a favorable sender reputation.
- Authentication: Setting up authentication protocols such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC is crucial for email warm-up. These protocols enhance your email deliverability and provide an extra layer of security, further improving your sender reputation.
### 6 Best Practices to Do Email Warm Up The Right Way
1. Segment Your Email List
  By tailoring your warm-up strategy to each segment, you can optimize engagement and maintain a positive sender reputation.
2. Start Slowly
   Begin with a small number of emails per day and gradually increase the volume.
   For example, you might start sending out a welcome email to new subscribers, then two or three emails a week to a small segment of your email list. Over time, you can slowly increase the frequency. Maybe you send emails to 25% of your list the first week, 45% the second week, and so forth.
3. Vary Email Types
   The goal at this stage is to connect with your customers or prospects.
4. Monitor Email Engagement Metrics
5. Avoid Spam Traps
   Spam traps are email addresses set up by IPS to identify and catch senders who engage in poor email practices or send unsolicited emails. They can be either pristine (never used by real individuals) or recycled (formerly active addresses). Hitting spam traps can harm your sender’s reputation
6. Improve Authentication Protocols
   Authentication is essential for email warm-up as it enhances deliverability.
   The first step is to set up the Sender Policy Framework (SPF), which allows you to specify which servers are authorized to send emails on behalf of your domain.
   The next step is to implement DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM), which adds a digital signature to your outgoing emails.
   Finally, consider implementing Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC).
