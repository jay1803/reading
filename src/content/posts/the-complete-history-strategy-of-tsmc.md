## Overview
This is a transcript of an Acquired podcast episode on TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company). The hosts, Ben Gilbert and David Rosenthal, discuss the history, business model, and strategic importance of TSMC, highlighting its rise to become the world's leading semiconductor foundry and its critical role in the global technology landscape. They analyze TSMC's powers, its geopolitical significance, and its impact on the broader semiconductor industry.

- **TSMC's Founding and Rise:** Morris Chang founded TSMC at age 56, and the company has since become the ninth-largest in the world, with its market cap doubling since 2021 to over a trillion dollars. TSMC's success is attributed to Chang's vision of a pure-play foundry model, focusing solely on manufacturing and enabling the rise of fabless chip design companies.

- **Key Players and Events:** The episode covers the early careers of Morris Chang and Don Valentine, the invention of the integrated circuit by Jack Kilby and Robert Noyce, and the pivotal decision by IBM to choose Intel over Texas Instruments for the IBM PC processor. It also discusses the founding of ARM in 1987 and the formation of P.A. Semi, which later became the foundation for Apple's silicon.

- **TSMC's Competitive Advantages:** TSMC possesses significant scale economies and process power, making it difficult for competitors to catch up. The company's close relationships with equipment manufacturers like ASML and its ability to invest heavily in R&D and CapEx have solidified its leading position in the industry.

- **Geopolitical Significance and Risks:** TSMC's location in Taiwan raises geopolitical concerns due to the ongoing tensions between Taiwan and China. The episode explores the potential consequences of a Chinese annexation of Taiwan and the importance of onshoring semiconductor manufacturing capabilities in other regions.

- **The Apple Deal:** Morris Chang returned as CEO in 2009 and secured a deal with Apple in 2012, which involved TSMC investing $9 billion to build a dedicated plant for Apple. This partnership was a bet-the-farm move for both companies and solidified TSMC's position as the leading manufacturer for Apple's chips.

- **Financial Performance and Future Outlook:** TSMC has demonstrated impressive financial performance, with a 17.4% compound annual revenue growth since its IPO in 1994 and a 19.9% IRR. The company's ability to generate high operating margins and reinvest profits into CapEx has allowed it to stay ahead of the competition.
