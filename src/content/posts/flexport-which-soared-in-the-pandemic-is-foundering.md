### Be very a-freight
San Francisco-based Flexport was founded in 2013 to bring order to the fragmented global supply chain. Its software allows companies to route cargo from manufacturers and onto container ships, planes, trains or trucks and to save money by doing it all efficiently and fast.

“Optimizing supply chains” isn’t typically the stuff of internet fame, yet the tech press covered Flexport closely, and founder Ryan Petersen was on the [cover of Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alexkonrad/2022/02/07/flexport-is-silicon-valleys-solution-to-the-supply-chain-mess-why-do-insiders-hope-it-sinks/). That’s partly because prominent venture capital firms like Andreessen Horowitz, Founders Fund and SoftBank piled into the startup, lifting its valuation to $8 billion last year, and partly because Petersen himself is a charismatic spokesperson with a knack for commanding attention on Twitter (now X) and in the media. During the supply chain crisis in fall 2021, for example, Petersen chartered a boat at the Port of Long Beach and [tweeted about the backup](https://twitter.com/typesfast/status/1451543776992845834?lang=en) of ships offshore.

In June 2022, Petersen poached former Amazon.com Inc. logistics czar Dave Clark, one of the tech giant’s highest profile execs, to take over as chief executive officer.

There were news stories about a [personality clash](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/flexport-founder-ousts-ceo-dave-clark-and-top-lieutenants) between the two execs, and Clark gave an interview to The Information saying he [was effectively fired](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/dave-clark-says-ouster-from-flexport-was-a-surprise-to-him) while hinting he might run for governor of Texas in 2026.
### Cost of Shipping a Container From China to US West Coast

The company commanded 0.46% of total US seaborne imports at the start of the pandemic in January 2020 and peaked under Clark at 1.15% this February, according to data from S&P Global Market Intelligence Panjiva. Since then, its market share has headed downward, to 1.06% in August.

But then the pandemic ended, e-commerce sales plateaued and Flexport’s revenue — propping up that $8 billion valuation set during the boom — started to fall. With that set of pungent facts at hand, Flexport apparently decided it had to return to profitability, fast.

The same pandemic hangover is affecting retail companies everywhere, from [Instacart Inc](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-09-06/instacart-prepares-to-set-ipo-price-range-as-early-as-monday). to Amazon, which lost 51% of its market value in 2022, and Target, down 30% this year. At Flexport, the reckoning has just been a little more entertaining to watch.
