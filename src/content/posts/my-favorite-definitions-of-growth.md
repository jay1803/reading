### Growth Hacking
If you actually listen to an interview with him or check out his 2017 book, Hacking Growth, you’ll see that he frames Growth in all the right ways:
- Focus on the scientific method / experimentation - “The best way to think about growth hacking is that it is a scientific method to analyze, test, and learn from all your projects. It can be applied at any company. What works changes year to year, but the process by which you figure out what works hasn't changed.”
- Emphasis on learning - “The companies that grow the fastest are the ones that learn the fastest.”
- Not limited to acquisition - “Remember that a core tenet of growth hacking is experimentation all through the customer experience funnel: not just customer awareness and acquisition but also activation, retention, revenue, and referral.”
- Inherently cross-functional - “Growth is not just a concern of sales and marketing, but of product, engineering and support too. It is this organization-wide commitment to growth that ultimately sets these companies apart.”
- Long-term mentality - “Your goal should be to ensure long-term sustainable growth, not just quick, short-term gains.”

### Growth teams chase business performance.
I heard this one from Lauryn Motamedi, who runs Growth at Notion.

Here’s how she looks at it: ‘My team chases business performance. We don’t decide in advance what we’re going to do… if business goes down here, we go and swarm there. If the business goes up, we see if our resources could be used elsewhere.’

The focus is on helping the company to achieve its goals in the highest leverage ways, at any given time. Instead of doing a lot of future planning, the Growth team can pick up and switch to something else, even if it’s in the middle of the week.

One note: This approach is a much better fit for younger, smaller Growth teams.
### Your core product and marketing teams are building a highway for customer value. Your Growth teams are building on-ramps onto that highway.
heard from Hemal Shah, who runs Growth at Atlassian

“Imagine that your product is a highway for cars (your customers). The customers’ objective is to reach their destination (solving their problem). The core team is responsible for building the main highway: a direct route, smooth road with no potholes, clear markings, and many lanes to scale.
Now, outside of the highway, many other paths exist: some are dirt roads, some are dead ends, or slow local roads with many stop signs. The Growth team’s objective is to find the customers on these alternative paths and build on-ramps to the main highway.”

This framework connects particularly well with Bangaly Kaba’s brilliant Adjacent User Theory. Essentially, after your highway is good, it’s the Growth team’s goal to bring on not only the same type of cars (i.e. customers), but adjacent users—different types of vehicles, say semi trailers (enterprises!), onto the highway. Ultimately, a very mature Growth team’s position is to expand PMF.
### Growth is the ‘sales team’ for self-serve revenue.
This is one is mine and it is particularly helpful for Product-Led growth businesses that have self-serve revenue.

In product-led growth companies, where a large portion of the revenue comes via self-serve channels, Growth teams take on the largest responsibility: owning self-serve revenue targets. In this world, the Growth team is really just a ‘sales’ team for self-serve. Growth teams here don’t have traditional sales teams structures… instead, they consist of product, design, data, and engineering resources that work to optimize product experience so the product can ‘sell itself.’ In this world, they are often not even bucketed under traditional R&D—it’s a GTM function that is resourced with R&D.
### Conclusion
Although it would be nice to have one simple, clean definition, I think each of these provides important context:
- The “how” of Growth: Sean calling out the focus on experimentation and cross-functional involvement
- The objective of Growth: Casey talking about distributing value and Lauryn talking about chasing business value.
- Understanding Growth by analogy: Hemal comparing it to on-ramps, and me comparing it to sales, but for self-serve.
