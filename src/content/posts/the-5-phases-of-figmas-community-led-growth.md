## Summary
1. **Introduction**: Claire Butler, Senior Director of Marketing at Figma, discusses Figma's community-led growth phases and offers advice for startups on building organic growth.

2. **Early Days and Stealth Mode**: Claire joined Figma during its stealth mode and was the first business hire, focusing on marketing and planning the launch strategy.

3. **Planting Seeds**: Initial phase where the focus was on building relationships with potential users, getting feedback, and involving them in the product development journey.

4. **Building Credibility**: Figma built credibility by engaging deeply with the design community, using authentic communication, and focusing on creating valuable content.

5. **Early Evangelism**: Leveraging community support to get early adopters to try Figma, flipping skeptics into advocates, and building a core group of enthusiastic users.

6. **Empowering Evangelists**: Expanding the group of evangelists and introducing self-serve revenue, allowing users to bring Figma into their organizations organically.

7. **Enterprise and Scale**: Introducing a sales team four years after launch to support larger organizations, addressing security and procurement needs, and formalizing enterprise strategies.

8. **Community Strategy**: Figma’s community approach involved personal outreach, building trust through feedback, and maintaining long-term relationships with early users.

9. **Content Marketing**: Figma used highly technical and passionate content to build credibility, with designers and engineers sharing deep insights about the product.

10. **Leveraging Twitter**: Figma targeted "design Twitter" for distribution and engagement, using analytical tools to map out influential designers and build relationships with them.

11. **Adapting to Feedback**: Figma's approach involved constant iteration based on user feedback, with a focus on understanding and addressing designers' needs.

12. **Scaling Community Engagement**: As Figma grew, it continued to prioritize community engagement, using advocates and real-time feedback to refine its product and marketing strategies.

13. **Introducing Pricing**: Figma introduced a pricing model two years after launch, focusing on team collaboration features while maintaining a free tier to encourage widespread adoption.

14. **Sales Team Introduction**: The sales team was introduced four years after launch to help scale enterprise adoption, focusing on supporting internal champions within organizations.

15. **Organic Growth and Inbound Leads**: Figma’s growth relied heavily on organic inbound leads and user advocacy, minimizing the need for aggressive outbound sales tactics.

16. **Lessons Learned**: Reflecting on the journey, Claire discusses the balance between building credibility, fostering community, and scaling the product effectively.
