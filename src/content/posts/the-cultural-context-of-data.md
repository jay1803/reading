The best book I’ve read on this topic in the recent past is called The Secret of our Success by Joseph Henrich. The book isn’t brand new (2015), but it was new to me. Wikipedia’s summary of Henrich’s research interests is pithy:
> [Henrich] is interested in the question of how humans evolved from "being a relatively unremarkable primate a few million years ago to the most successful species on the globe", and how culture shaped our species' genetic evolution.
### Gene-culture coevolution
The big idea in the book is “gene-culture coevolution”:
> Human characteristics are the product of gene–culture coevolution, which is an evolutionary dynamic involving the interaction of genes and culture over long time periods.
Fire is a physical process, but starting a fire, keeping it going, and cooking are all culture.

But the fact that we can do all of these things, in turn shapes us at the most basic level of all: our genes. In the ~100,000 years that we’ve had fire and cooking, our jaws have changed, our teeth have changed, and most importantly, our guts have changed.

Gene-culture coevolution. Culture is not a layer on top of genes, it is hard-wired into who we are. There is a feedback loop.
### Credibility is central
If we can no longer survive without our culture, and cultural knowledge has to be passed down, then the act of passing down cultural knowledge is critical for our survival.

We’re also familiar with this at the individual level. Each one of us has felt the pressure to succeed as children, in school, in college…get good grades, get into a good school, learn quickly on the job...we’re all afraid of is being left out in the cold…outside the warm glow of the cultural fire.
#### In cultural knowledge transmission, credibility is everything.
Many foods don’t immediately kill you—this would be easy to observe. Many foods cause long-term health or nutritional problems, and it’s possible that no single individual has ever been able to truly see the cause-and-effect relationship between one thing and the other. Food prohibitions are arrived at by experimentation, guesses, and, ultimately, empirical success (survival).
This is just one example, but this extends to countless things about our cultural knowledge. Success is hard to directly observe, and causality is hard to assess, but culture evolves over hundreds of generations towards successful solutions.

Assessing an individual’s credibility is, then, critical to survival. Trusting the wrong person to act as a mentor could be a deadly decision. And so we are wired to do this nearly from birth.
Babies even differentiate between whom they trust in what domains!
We are constantly, constantly, evaluating each others’ credibility. We cannot avoid it—it is literally central to who we are as a species.
### Personal vs. institutional credibility
There are fundamentally two things that can have credibility: individuals or institutions.
Perhaps the biggest change in human cultural evolution in the past couple of thousand years has been about transitioning the primary trust / credibility relationships from human-to-human to human-to-institution.

Human-to-human trust networks don’t scale beyond a certain point and are as fragile as the people in them (i.e. very fragile).

Human-to-institution trust networks scale and can be made more robust than any individual human.

Here’s the punchline. dbt asks people to move from human-to-human trust networks to human-to-institution trust networks.

Prior to dbt, the primary way that data was passed around a company was emailed spreadsheets. When you get a spreadsheet in an email, the credibility in that spreadsheet flows directly from the human sending it to you.
Those of us who produce those tables understand and trust the process.
To those on the outside, understanding of this new institution is still low.

It’s simply a recognition that the shift to the modern data stack is not only a technical transition, it’s a cultural transition.

### 什么是数据传播和信任问题
数据传播和信任的问题是指，当数据从生产者到消费者的过程中，如何保证数据的准确性、完整性和可靠性。数据传播和信任的问题涉及到数据的来源、处理、存储、分析、展示和使用等方面。数据传播和信任的问题在现代数据堆栈中尤为突出，因为数据堆栈涉及到多种技术、工具和平台，以及多种角色、职责和利益。数据传播和信任的问题对于数据分析工程领域的发展和应用有着重要的影响。
### 文章中提到的个人到个人的信任网络是指什么
文章中提到的个人到个人的信任网络是指，数据的传播和信任主要依赖于数据生产者和消费者之间的个人关系。例如，当一个人收到一份电子邮件中的电子表格时，他或她对这份表格的信任程度取决于发送者的信誉。个人到个人的信任网络是一种基于人类基因-文化共进化的机制，可以帮助人们评估知识的可信度。但是，个人到个人的信任网络也有其局限性，例如不能很好地扩展，容易受到个人因素的影响。
### 什么是基因文化共同进化机制
基因文化共同进化机制是指，人类的基因和文化是相互影响、相互作用的两个进化系统。人类的基因决定了人类的生理和心理特征，而人类的文化决定了人类的行为和知识。人类的基因和文化可以相互选择、相互适应、相互改变，从而促进了人类的进化。基因文化共同进化机制是一种解释人类复杂性和多样性的理论框架。
### 跟 Dual inheritance theory 是什么关系
基因文化共同进化机制和双重遗传理论是同一个概念的不同说法。双重遗传理论是指，人类的进化受到两种遗传系统的影响：基因遗传系统和文化遗传系统。基因遗传系统是指，人类通过生物学的方式将基因从一代传递到下一代。文化遗传系统是指，人类通过社会学的方式将文化从一代传递到下一代。双重遗传理论认为，基因和文化是相互依存、相互制约、相互促进的两个进化力量。
