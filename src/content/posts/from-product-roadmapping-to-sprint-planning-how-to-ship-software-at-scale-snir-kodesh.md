## 概述

This article is a transcript of a podcast interview with Snir Kodesh, Head of Engineering at Retool. The conversation covers various topics related to managing and scaling engineering teams, product development processes, and the differences between consumer and enterprise software development. Kodesh shares insights on product roadmapping, sprint planning, team organization, and leadership principles.

## 主题

- 🤝 **Collaboration between Product and Engineering:**
Retool emphasizes a highly collaborative approach between product and engineering teams. This collaboration is crucial because Retool is a highly technical product, and engineering needs to be deeply involved in understanding and shaping the product experience. Retool's debug tools are a product consideration, and solving its challenges effectively is considered a developer and engineer's problem.

- 📅 **Planning Process and Cadence:**
Retool plans to the half-year but recalibrates to the quarter. They shifted from OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) to a QBR (Quarterly Business Review) format to accommodate longer-term projects that can span more than a quarter. The QBR process involves subjective assessments of team progress and involves a core team plus leaders from product, engineering and design functions.

- 🎯 **QBRs vs. OKRs:**
Retool uses QBRs instead of OKRs because QBRs are a more subjective and directional assessment. It's a system to think about progress. The teams score themselves. It allows for more open-ended thinking and doesn’t force teams to focus solely on metrics that might not be achievable or relevant within a quarter. The QBR meeting focuses on three points: What could have gone better? What went well? What are going to be your plans going forward?.

- ⚠️ **Estimation and Predictability:**
Kodesh discusses estimate struggles, admitting there's no silver bullet. He suggests moving more thought earlier in the process to mitigate risks and improve estimation accuracy. He mentions a "bug bash" process to improve quality and find issues before shipping, which can impact estimations admissibility.

- 📈 **Defining Excellent Performance:**
For engineering teams, excellent performance comes down to impact and the way that impact is achieved (the cultural side). It’s important how motivated the team members are, if there is a high degree of autonomy and respect and if they understand the motivation of the work. Excellent Performance is not a lines of code metric.

- 🧑‍💻 **Engineering Leadership at Scale:**
When helping engineering leaders scale their teams, Kodesh emphasizes the importance of delegation and strategic hiring. He shares that founders need to make sure that they are getting great organizational leaders and not only 1% technical leaders. He advises using counterfactual thinking to help leaders recognize the value of delegating and making strategic hires.

- ⚖️ **Balancing Enterprise and SMB Needs:**
Retool organizes its engineering team around customer challenges. They balance between enterprise and Startups needs, recognizing that enterprise customers have distinct needs related to integration and scaling Retool within their organizations, while also focusing on the core product experience that benefits all users.

- 🧭 **Consumer vs. Enterprise Product Development:**
Kodesh compares his experience at Lyft (consumer) and Retool (enterprise), highlighting the difference in product complexity. Enterprise platforms like Retool have infinitely many permutations of use cases, making observability and operations more challenging. The challenge with Retool is creating a client-side tool that supports infinite branch factor in a scalable and secure way.

- 🗣️ **"Try, Do, Consider" Framework:**
This framework helps structure feedback and communication between leadership and teams. "Do" indicates a strong directive, "Consider" is more exploratory, and "Try" suggests an experiment or loosely held opinion.
