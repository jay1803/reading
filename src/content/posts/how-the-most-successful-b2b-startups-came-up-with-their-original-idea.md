### 概述
This article is part one of a seven-part series about kickstarting and scaling a B2B business. It focuses on how successful B2B startups came up with their original ideas. The author identifies three main paths for discovering a great idea: past pain, ponder and probe, and present pull.
### 主题
- 💡 **Three elements of a great B2B startup idea:** There needs to be a lot of people willing to spend a lot of money to solve the problem. Existing solutions need to be doing a subpar job of solving the problem, and the problem needs to be something you are willing to spend years of your life solving.

- 🔍 **Pain and Pull**: No matter which path you take, you are looking for two things: pain and pull. Pain tells you there's an opportunity to solve a problem, and that it's important. Pull tells you that you’re actually solving the problem.

- 🔄 **Pivoting is common**: About 40% of startups pivoted at least once before landing on their winning idea—oftentimes more than once. Many founders had no special skill or background in the problem space they went after.

- 🗣️ **Customer interviews are essential**: Founders spoke to a median of 30 potential customers to validate their idea before committing. This process helps to determine market desire and to refine the potential solution.

- 🛠️ **Past Pain:** This involves identifying a large pain point experienced at a previous company and building a solution. Examples include Gong, Retool, Linear, Persona, and Hex. The founders often found themselves repeatedly building similar internal tools or facing the same unresolved issues, which indicated an unmet need in the market.

- 🤔 **Ponder and Probe:** This path involves picking a space of interest, whiteboarding, tinkering, and talking to potential customers to find pain and pull. Companies like Figma, Vanta, Snowflake, and Notion followed this path. They often explored multiple ideas and pivoted based on customer feedback, focusing on emerging trends and underserved markets.

- 🌱 **Present Pull:** This occurs when a feature or functionality built shows a strong pull from existing users, leading to a potential pivot or a completely new product. Companies such as Amplitude, Sprig, Segment, and Slack discovered their core idea by noticing existing customers using a tool or feature in unexpected ways, providing positive feedback indicating clear market pull.

- 🌟 **Prosumer Products Journey**: Every prosumer product (e.g., Notion, Figma, Airtable, Miro, Slack, Coda) took two to four years of wandering before finding something successful.
