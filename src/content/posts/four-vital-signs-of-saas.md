## The Four Vital Signs of SaaS
The Vital Signs of SaaS are
- Revenue Growth
  Revenue Growth matters because growth is the central purpose of a startup
- Sales Efficiency
- Revenue Churn
- Cash Burn
## Measuring and Benchmarking the Four Vital Signs of SaaS
### Vital Sign #1: Revenue Growth
ARR Growth Rate
use [Mendoza Line](https://www.scalevp.com/blog/understanding-the-mendoza-line-for-saas-growth) for SaaS growth that tracks at or just above the bottom quartile of growth rates

Another way to look at the same data is to think about the revenue trajectory over time, or “how many years to $100M”.

An example is the [T2D3](https://www.battery.com/powered/helping-entrepreneurs-triple-triple-double-double-double-to-a-billion-dollar-company/) (triple-triple-double-double-double) rule, which is also shown in the table above.
### Vital Sign #2: Sales Efficiency
Sales Efficiency metrics (and again the reminder to think of this more broadly as Distribution Efficiency!) measure the relationship between dollars in (spent on Sales and Marketing) and dollars out (in the form of new revenue).

For a Vital Signs diagnosis, we prefer this metric to the complexity of the LTV-CAC Ratio

We call this formula using GAAP instead of ARR the [Magic Number](https://www.scalevp.com/blog/magic-number-math) and it should be equal to Net Sales Efficiency with a one quarter lag (to allow ARR to convert to GAAP).

The median Magic Number for our dataset is between 0.8x and 0.7x, and the range from top quartile to bottom quartile is between 0.5x and 1.5x.
### Vital Sign #3: Revenue Churn
The simplest way to measure Gross and Net Churn  is by taking Churned ARR (Gross) and Churned less Upsell ARR (Net) and dividing it by opening ARR for the period, usually a quarter.

At a $20M revenue run rate even bottom quartile companies have annualized Gross Churn hovering around -21%.

### Vital Sign #4: Cash Burn / Operating Income
cash burn by looking at free Cash Flow for a quarter (operating cashflow less capex) and compare it to cash on the balance sheet, to calculate a cash out date.

To illustrate what this metric means, at a $20M revenue run rate the median company in the dataset is losing 63% of revenue, $12.7M dollars, or colloquially “burning $1M a month”.
